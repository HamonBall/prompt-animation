The main problem is a mismatch between **the plan’s intended movement and the motion the rig actually performs**. The project already has staged actions and contact checks, but it still treats many actions as a series of poses. That leaves room for the falling, floating, and stiff transitions you’ve seen.

1. **Stages can still look like separate pose blends.** The motion builder eases each stage’s controls independently, then bakes them as linear keys. Sequential stages can stop before the next begins, and there’s no shared velocity or momentum across the sequence. That can make movement feel robotic or ragged even when the plan has sensible stages. See [compose_frame](C:/work11/src/avatar_app/blender/motion.py:194) and [key interpolation](C:/work11/src/avatar_app/blender/motion.py:163).

2. **Some plans omit the support needed for the action.** The built-in lying-down example has a “sit on floor” stage with no contacts, followed by a stage that rotates the body and lowers its pelvis. The model can produce similar plans. A saved review found about **15 cm of floor penetration before repair**; after repair, the final penetration was about **3 mm**, while peak joint speed reached **829°/s**. That candidate passed. The report is useful, but passing it doesn’t mean the movement looks convincing. See the [reference plan](C:/work11/src/avatar_app/planning/movements.py:54) and [saved review](C:/work11/artifacts/motion/review.json).

3. **“Sit on a chair” has no chair in the app’s preview scene.** The static-pose interpreter can describe a chair-like sitting pose with no seat contact; the staged planner is told to ask for clarification when the needed surface hasn’t been resolved. The UI provides no way to add or select a seat, and the preview scene has no visible floor. So a pose can look as though it’s floating even when it has been lowered to the invisible floor. See [pose instructions](C:/work11/src/avatar_app/planning/pose_interpreter.py:54), [surface handling](C:/work11/src/avatar_app/planning/action_interpreter.py:67), and [preview setup](C:/work11/src/avatar_app/blender/scene.py:5).

Contact positions are measured separately, but target completion can be a false pass if the rig or solver didn’t reach the intended pose. See [sample recording](C:/work11/src/avatar_app/blender/motion.py:358) and [completion check](C:/work11/src/avatar_app/blender/motion.py:104). ## Implemented — 2026-10-12

- Adjacent continuing controls share monotone Hermite tangents. Direction reversals stop smoothly; contact acquisition and release have separate ramps. Body support ramps start from their entry position rather than accumulating their displacement every frame.
- Floor sitting, supported lowering, and lying down use explicit pelvis/back contacts and timed hand support. Support height uses evaluated skin, including the changing body orientation. Hands stay clear during release. Unspecified lower limbs still inherit their upper limb's movement.
- The preview has a visible floor and a real bounded seat with height controls. Seat placement accounts for footwear and places the pelvis on the forward half, keeping descending thighs clear of the edge. Successful floor-only support routes clear the unused preview seat.
- Review checks actual rig rotations, limb directions, wrist/pelvis coordinates, evaluated floor/seat penetration, skin support gaps, and contact acquisition. Limits are 360°/s speed, 3000°/s² vector angular acceleration, 3 mm penetration, and 25 mm contact error. Failed or canceled builds restore the previous action and scene state.
- Simple sitting and lying requests use reviewed routes without local-model inference. Creative requests continue through the general planner. Generated lying routes receive destination/support checks; the model's example uses the same route as the executor tests. Successful actions retain their measured ending state for subsequent requests.
- The UI adds experimental **Try anyway**, **Restore reviewed result**, actual inference phases/elapsed time/activity, and cancelable frame-by-frame construction. Optional public HTTPS reference text provides internet context; it is off by default and does not send the prompt or avatar to the website.
- A procedural rigged avatar works without personal FBX files. Project Markdown is trackable; generated output, model binaries, personal media, and `problems/` stay ignored.
