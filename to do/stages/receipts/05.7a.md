## Transition and hold

Demo passed the 43-frame neutral-entry pilot with a 5° yaw over 1 s and a
0.4 s terminal hold. Both complete frame sequences were inspected. The
front frames show a small head turn followed by a steady endpoint. The
profile shows the torso and legs staying aligned through the hold. The
approval is recorded against the saved motion and media hashes. The 43-frame
Mixamo case shows the same small yaw and hold with the turned crouch preserved.
The [current-source range bakes](#current-source-range-bakes) cover the short
and long cases on both rigs.

## Alternating strokes

Both rigs completed one finite pair of alternating arm strokes in 172 frames.
The cases use a 1.2 s phase, 0.1 s hold and 40 mm stroke amplitude. The delayed
hand starts from a held measured endpoint, and the quintic easing keeps the
approach within the existing forearm acceleration limit. Maximum hand tracking
error was 0.3 µm. Both front views show the lead changing arms before both
return to entry. The side views show the elbow lift and progressive return.
Demo starts from neutral and Mixamo from a turned crouch. Both preserve the
floor soles. The result uses finite playback.

## Translating steps

Both rigs completed the 121-frame, two-step cycle from a turned shallow crouch
with 60 mm of forward displacement and 40 mm authored clearance. Demo leads
left and Mixamo leads right. Front and side views show the swing alternating
between soles, feet clearing the floor and replanting, and a short forward
pelvis advance. Both sequences preserve the upper-body alignment. The gait
is slow and restrained, with a short stride.

After compensating for the declared translation, Demo's measured seam had
0.000007° pose residual, 0.000042 mm endpoint residual and zero velocity
difference at the reporting precision. Mixamo measured 0.54410°, 1.55632 mm
and 0.00026°/s angular velocity difference. The cycle metadata preserves
the entry heading and matches the held sole identities at the seam.

A separate native test reopened both saved bundles and built a second cycle
from the measured exit, with 121 frames per rig. The pelvis matched the
measured exit at the start to reporting precision. Travel-vector error on the
continued cycle was below 0.1 µm on Demo and 1.5561 mm on Mixamo. The head
orientation remained within 0.00001°. Both rejected the original finite
cycle's stale entry and preserved its action and frame on rejection.

## Capability boundaries

The [fragment contract](../../../docs/contracts/fragment-execution-v1.md#stage-057a-timing-and-finite-repeat)
specifies the per-ID defaults, bounds, frames, ownership and exit states.
The [coverage ledger](../movement-coverage.md) maps each ID to its reviewed
envelope and the cases above. Generic operator duration and repeat limits are
contract tests. The visible yaw and repeat examples exercise the shorter
durations and counts in the ledger.

Traveling steps require reachable captured floor contacts. A straight-leg
Demo setup rejected the low-clearance variant during leg-reach validation.
The loop proof is for translation with unchanged heading. The authored step
and support checks cover placement and continuity under the tested entries.
Undulation follow-through requires a calibrated upper arm on the selected
side. The capability test verifies explicit rejection when it is missing.

The schema-v1 easing enum and generated schemas now include
`smootherstep`. Existing linear and smooth motion retain their samplers.
Timing and patterns flow through the typed single-fragment path. General
composition, style and model-driven execution remain later-stage work.
README updates are deferred under the project freeze.
