## First implementation

`support.acquire_release` and `support.transfer` have an initial
profile-8 sole implementation in `movement_library/support.py`. The
shared schedule uses the existing typed fragment and frame grid. It
retains fixed entry support through the new contact's acquisition and
hold before beginning release. Releasing the last contact requires
explicit airborne intent. The initial adapter blends a calibrated sole
point toward the fixed world target through the existing leg IK. The
solved snapshot carries fully held anchors and their surface identities.

`ContactEvent.target_world_v1` is an additive world-metre target.
Omitting it preserves legacy decoding and serialized payloads. The five
generated schemas that reference ContactEvent now expose the field.
The builder checks resolved surface height and bounds. Distinct
attachments to the same anchor are rejected if both own an overlapping
interval or a shared sampled endpoint. The initial fixed-target adapter
accepts sole contacts in dedicated contact-only fragments.

Acquisition, hold and release default to 0.8, 0.2 and 0.8 s when used.
Acquisition and release each allow 0.5–2 s and hold allows 0.1–1 s.
Phases are aligned to 30 fps. Either sole can be selected. The target
defaults to the entry sole's horizontal location on the resolved floor,
or can be an explicit world point on a resolved horizontal surface.
The initial definitions own the thigh, shin and ankle chains.

## Geometric balance

`body.balance` now authors a slow horizontal pelvis approach over one
captured floor sole. The endpoint defaults to the measured midpoint of
the heel and forefoot. The balance proxy is a rectangle along their
horizontal axis with a half-width of 15% of its length. The projected
pelvis is an approximate body center. The target needs 5 mm inward
margin, a shift no longer than 220 mm, and unchanged height. The
approach lasts 1.2–3 s (default 1.5 s) and the hold lasts 0.2–1 s
(default 0.5 s). The opposite sole must stay at least 20 mm above the
floor. The settled projection and contact residual are measured from
the solved pose. These checks describe slow geometric support.

## Reviewed movement IDs

The six published examples cover the three IDs on Demo and Mixamo
with profile 8. The [fixture manifest](../../../tests/fixtures/support_transfers/v1/manifest.json)
links the parameter schemas, entry snapshots, executables and review
records. The publication summary links all 38 cases and 3,044 motion
samples to their accepted motion and media identities.

### support.acquire_release

18 clips and 1,530 motion samples cover sole, heel, forefoot, palm,
pelvis, back and knee cycles. The default is a left-sole cycle on the
floor. Acquire, hold and release default to 0.8, 0.2 and 0.8 s. The
limits are 0.5–2 s for the ramps and 0.1–1 s for the hold. A target is
the entry reference projected onto the resolved horizontal surface or
an explicit world point on that surface. The movement preserves the
solved end. It owns both leg chains, the calibrated palm arm chains
and pelvis where body anchors are available. Releasing the last
support requires explicit airborne intent.

### support.transfer

12 clips and 942 motion samples cover sole transfer and transfers
from feet to palm, pelvis and knee. The default transfers the right
sole to the left sole on the floor. The target, ramp and hold ranges,
owned chains and solved-end policy follow acquire/release. The old
support stays held through acquisition and hold of the new anchor.
The compatibility checks cover unreachable targets and overlapping
ownership.

### body.balance

Eight clips and 572 motion samples cover both stance sides and the
timing extremes. The default approaches the measured left-sole
footprint midpoint in 1.5 s (1.2–3 s) and holds for 0.5 s (0.2–1 s).
Entry requires one captured floor sole and at least 20 mm clearance
under the other sole. The target must preserve pelvis height, stay
within 220 mm of the entry projection and finish at least 5 mm
inside the support region. The movement owns the pelvis and both leg
chains and preserves the solved end. The recorded margin is geometric.
